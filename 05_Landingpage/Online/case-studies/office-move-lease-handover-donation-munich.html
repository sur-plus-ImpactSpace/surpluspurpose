Six weeks to the strip-out deadline

How a Munich software company moving from three floors to one donated 180 workstations - planned backwards from the handover date, without a clearance firm and without interim storage.

Illustrative example. This scenario shows a typical trigger and the process the platform is built for. Companies and recipients are deliberately unnamed; figures are illustrative and follow the Impact Calculator methodology (GHG Protocol, GIIN IRIS+, tax effect versus disposal). Real cases will replace these examples as soon as pilot partners release them.

The trigger

A software company with 260 employees moves from three floors to one. The old lease ends on 30 November - premises back broom-clean, fit-out removed. What does not move with them: 180 workstations with desk and chair, 24 meeting tables, 90 pedestals, 40 cabinets, two kitchenettes. Replacement value around €210,000, roughly 18 tonnes.

The clearance firm quoted €14,500: everything out in two days, recovery unspecified. The alternative, interim storage for 120 square metres, would have cost €4,300 over three months - and only postponed the problem.

“The date was in the lease. Everything else was negotiable.”

- Head of Workplace, software company

The process

Eight weeks before the handover the company listed the surplus as a fixed-price clearance project - three lots: workstations, meeting rooms, kitchen and cabinets. Planning ran backwards from the deadline:

  • Weeks 8 to 6: listing with quantities and photos to the template. Personally coordinated matching: an education provider, two associations, a non-profit advice centre - five matches, because the education provider equipped two sites.
  • Weeks 3 and 2: four collection days. The logistics partner dismantled and transported at cost, €5,200. Handover record per collection.
  • Week 1: only the fit-out left for the strip-out. Handover on 30 November with no claim from the landlord.

The honest maths

Four-year-old office furniture has a second-hand value of around five percent. VAT is due on that, around €2,000; the deduction recovers around €600. Net tax effect about minus €1,400. The case still pays clearly: €14,500 clearance and €4,300 storage avoided, €5,200 logistics and the tax effect netted off - around €12,000 before the sur.plus fixed price.

“We took 60 workstations to the new floor. The other 180 now stand in rooms where there was nothing before.”

- Head of Workplace, software company

Frequently asked questions

How much lead time does an office furniture donation need in a move?

Six to eight weeks before the handover is realistic: two weeks for listing and matches, two to three weeks for collections, one week of buffer for removing the fit-out.

Which is cheaper - clearance firm or donation?

For four-year-old furniture in good condition, usually the donation: clearance and interim storage fall away, logistics runs at cost. VAT on the second-hand value is small but is shown.
