General & matching

sur.plus handles non-perishable, usable goods: IT & electronics, office furniture, textiles & clothing, tools & machinery, stationery and general warehouse overstock. Items must be in usable condition.

Deliberately excluded to protect NGOs and people served: Food & beverages (cold-chain and food safety certification required) · Pharmaceuticals & medical devices (regulatory approval required) · Hazardous materials (EU-classified) · Weapons & controlled items (export control). These exclusions are a design choice, not a limitation.

In the current phase, matching is human-coordinated - Florian and the team personally review listings and make introductions. This ensures quality over speed and lets us build the proprietary transaction data that will train our matching engine. As listing volume grows, AI-assisted matching will activate across compatible categories. Every match, regardless of how it is made, requires confirmation from both sides before anything moves.

In the initial phase, sur.plus operates as a managed marketplace with a high-touch, concierge approach. Supply and demand are matched with close human involvement to ensure high success rates, build trust, and generate the proprietary transaction data that trains our engine. We start with office and facility surplus where disposal costs are high and NGO demand is immediate. Initial cases are documented as detailed case studies: cost savings, avoided CO₂ emissions, NGO impact - forming the foundation for scalable matching. This means you get a personal, hands-on experience from day one, not a self-service tool you have to figure out alone.

sur.plus does not run its own logistics. In most cases, the donating company arranges and covers transport - this is typically still cheaper than commercial disposal, and the cost is offset by savings on storage and disposal fees. We provide guidance on suitable carriers and, for Tree plan customers, can facilitate introductions to partners offering preferential rates for surplus transport. For very local matches (same city or region), NGOs often arrange collection themselves. Where logistics would otherwise prevent a match, we look for a solution together - talk to us before you decline.

sur.plus operates across Europe and beyond - EU member states, the UK, Norway, Switzerland, and as we expand, Global South markets. Cross-border shipments within the EU are straightforward - no customs declarations required for intra-EU transfers.

For non-EU countries (UK, Switzerland, Norway, and globally), customs declarations are required on both sides. The principles are similar regardless of country:

Non-EU shipments (UK, Switzerland, Norway and beyond): These countries are third countries for EU customs purposes. Shipments require declarations in both directions. Key points:

  • UK imports: UK Global Tariff applies; simplified procedures available for humanitarian shipments under HMRC charitable relief provisions
  • EU imports from UK: EU customs law applies; NGOs with recognised charitable status may qualify for duty relief
  • sur.plus provides all documentation needed for customs filing: item description, declared value, NGO details and donation certificate

Our recommendation: For cross-border shipments involving the UK or non-EU countries, work with a specialist freight forwarder. DHL GoGreen, Flexport and DSV all offer end-to-end customs handling for humanitarian shipments. We are happy to connect you with the right logistics partner - message us in the live chat.

sur.plus is not a customs broker or legal advisor. Always confirm specific customs treatment with your logistics partner or legal counsel.

You can always donate - the question is whether the tax deduction survives the border. The short version for the three most common cases:

  • German company → NGO in the EU/EEA: Since 1 January 2025, the donation deduction only applies if the foreign organisation is listed in the German Zuwendungsempfängerregister (donation recipient register) kept by the Federal Central Tax Office (Section 60b AO). Registered organisations may issue donation receipts in the official German format. No entry, no deduction. Worth telling your partners: organisations based in another EU/EEA state are not entered automatically, but can apply for entry themselves – electronically via the BZSt online portal. The BZSt examines whether the organisation meets the charitable-status requirements of German tax law; once approved, it may issue receipts in the official German format.
  • German company → NGO outside the EU/EEA (e.g. Switzerland, UK, India): no donation deduction in Germany. The donation is still possible and worthwhile - it just has no tax effect. VAT on the deemed supply applies regardless of where the recipient is based; for export and customs, see the logistics question above.
  • Vice versa - foreign company → German NGO: The donor country's tax law decides. Austria, for instance, requires entry in its list of favoured donation recipients, the Netherlands requires ANBI status - a German donation receipt alone is not enough there. From Switzerland, cross-border donations are generally not deductible at all.

That is why sur.plus prefers matching within the same country: clean tax treatment, shortest logistics, lowest CO₂ footprint. We only propose cross-border matches when deductibility has been clarified via register or status. The details belong with your tax advisor - we are not tax advisors.

We are not tax advisors - please consult your tax counsel for your specific situation and jurisdiction. That said, here is what is commonly used in practice across markets:

Common donation structures:
• Fully depreciated goods - VAT is assessed on the current notional purchase price, not the book value; for aged goods it is often low, and can be reduced under BMF guidance on limited marketability.
• Permanent loan (Dauerleihgabe) - company retains ownership, NGO has full use. Avoids VAT on ownership transfer.
• Structured via sur.plus - sur.plus is built to facilitate in-kind donations compliantly across EU member states, addressing the VAT complications that make disposal cheaper than giving.

Tax deduction on donations:
In many EU countries, documented in-kind donations to registered NGOs are tax-deductible - up to 20% of annual profit in Germany (§9 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 KStG). sur.plus generates a pre-filled (donation certificate) automatically after every match. The NGO confirms it digitally via one click - the signed document lands in your dashboard, ready for your tax advisor. This applies similarly in Austria, the Netherlands, Sweden and other EU markets. Specific limits and conditions vary by country.

Countries where VAT on in-kind donations is a known challenge:
• Germany - §3 Abs. 1b UStG: donations treated as taxable supplies if input VAT was previously deducted
• Austria - near-identical rule to Germany
• Poland, Czech Republic, Slovakia - no general exemption for in-kind donations
• Portugal, Greece, Romania, Bulgaria - very limited exemptions

Why this is not a German peculiarity:
Art. 16 of the EU VAT Directive (2006/112/EC) treats the disposal of business assets free of charge as a supply for consideration across the Union where input VAT was deducted; Art. 74 sets the notional purchase price as the taxable amount. Taxability is the rule, not the exception.

Where reliefs exist - and where they do not:
Almost all existing exemptions target new, unsold trading stock, not used fixed assets. France waives the input-VAT adjustment on donated unsold goods (Art. 273 septies D CGI, Loi AGEC), Belgium has a relief for unsold non-food goods, and many states exempt food donations. For decommissioned laptops, monitors and office furniture, none of this normally applies. Outside the EU, the United Kingdom introduced a relief on 1 April 2026 that also covers second-hand goods, with per-item value limits (£100, and £200 for computers and furniture). That gap - relief for new stock, tax on used goods - is exactly what our campaign is about.

With a genuine in-kind donation (legally: a gift), the donating company’s liability is strongly limited under German law: it is liable only for intent and gross negligence - and for damage caused by defects only where a defect was fraudulently concealed (secs. 521, 524 German Civil Code). A clean process still requires a safe handover: function and safety checks for electrical devices, plus GDPR-compliant data erasure for IT.

It is different for a sale at residual value (e.g. clearance): then sales law with warranty obligations applies. Between businesses (B2B) warranties can largely be limited by contract; when selling to consumers (B2C), consumer sales law applies - for used goods the warranty period can be shortened to one year but not excluded. Two points that are often missed: strict product liability falls on the manufacturer, not the donor - as long as the donor can name the manufacturer (sec. 4(3) German Product Liability Act); that is why manufacturer, model and serial number per device belong in the handover record. And risk passes on handover: a recipient collecting the goods bears it from the loading dock; a commissioned carrier is liable under secs. 425, 431 German Commercial Code. sur.plus documents condition, identity and handover for every match and provides handover protocol templates; we are not a party to the transfer and never take title to the goods. In German, with all five layers of liability and a real case: Haftung bei Sachspenden. We are not legal advisors - always clarify liability questions for your specific case with your counsel.

Exportable as PDF and structured data (JSON/CSV).

Every number sur.plus reports is built to be checked. Impact figures are clearly labelled as estimates, calculated with conservative factors under published methodologies (GHG Protocol avoided emissions, GIIN IRIS+ social resource value) - we report avoided emissions from reuse, never offset-based “climate neutral” claims. Every impact report and social media impact card carries its methodology label, date and match ID, so any figure can be traced back to a verified delivery. This matters from September 2026: under the EU Empowering Consumers Directive (2024/825), generic or unsubstantiated green claims become unlawful - sur.plus documentation is designed to give your claims the substantiation they will need.

From that date the Empowering Consumers Directive (2024/825) applies, in Germany through the amended UWG. Towards consumers, four kinds of statement become unlawful outright: generic environmental claims without proof (“sustainable”, “climate-friendly”, “green”), climate-neutrality claims based on offsetting, self-made sustainability labels, and vague future promises without a verifiable plan. Alongside that, the general ban on misleading advertising applies to every company, B2B included - competitors and associations can issue warning letters. What remains allowed is what is specific, quantified and substantiated: “120 laptops reused instead of scrapped, five non-profit recipients, avoided emissions calculated under GHG Protocol methodology, dated, outside our own inventory.” That is exactly how every sur.plus figure is built: method label, date and match ID sit on every impact report and every social media card, so the evidence exists before the claim is made. Which statements you publish is for your legal team to decide - we deliver the proof behind them.
