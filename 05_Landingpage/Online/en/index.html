04Why now

Idle surplus costs you three times.

In storage, in disposal and in the duty to report. Usable goods worth around €23B are destroyed in Europe every year.

€80-200
per m² and year is what idle surplus costs in warehouse storage alone
€50-200
per tonne on top for disposal - with no evidence and no impact
4 EU rules
demand evidence instead of intentions by 2029: ESPR, EmpCo, CSRD, CSDDD. What this means for companies →
·Advocacy

In 12+ EU member states, destroying surplus is cheaper than donating it.

End the VAT penalty on in-kind donations. Donate goods from business assets and you pay VAT as if you had sold them (a “deemed supply”) - destroying them triggers none. Remove that tax and the incentive to destroy usable goods disappears, the red tape ends, and donating becomes cheaper for companies and far easier for aid organisations. We’re calling on the EU to fix this at the source. How VAT on in-kind donations works →

05The story behind sur.plus

From Varanasi to
a table in Taiwan.

In Varanasi, I volunteered in a classroom that was short of pens, notebooks and school backpacks. 46 days later I sat in a ramen restaurant in Tainan, next to a Chinese leather-goods maker. She told me that returns with minor flaws get scrapped. Surplus bags.

The kids I'd bought school supplies for flashed into my head. So I asked her to find donation recipients for those returns.

This is no isolated case. Too much gets thrown away too soon - not out of bad will, but because donating takes too much effort.

Goodwill was never the problem. Effort is.

By category, yes - if you are looking for an in-kind donation platform for companies, this is it. We still call ourselves infrastructure, and the distinction is not word-play.

A platform provides a marketplace: supply in, demand in, the parties sort out the rest. That is precisely where in-kind donations fail - not for lack of goodwill, but because of the work in between. Who takes 40 desks, and by when? Is the organisation genuinely charitable? Who coordinates the handover, and how much of it survives an audit?

sur.plus does that work: recipient verification, coordinated matching, handover documentation and the evidence that tax and sustainability reporting require. Hence infrastructure - something you rely on, not something you operate.

sur.plus handles non-perishable, usable goods: IT & electronics, office furniture, textiles & clothing, tools & machinery, stationery and retail goods: returns, overstock, end-of-line and seasonal stock from retail and e-commerce. Retailers and online shops can pass on returns that can no longer be sold as donations instead of destroying them. Items must be in usable condition.

Deliberately excluded to protect NGOs and people served: Food & beverages (cold-chain and food safety certification required) · Pharmaceuticals & medical devices (regulatory approval required) · Hazardous materials (EU-classified) · Weapons & controlled items (export control). These exclusions are a design choice, not a limitation.

In the current phase, matching is human-coordinated - Florian and the team personally review listings and make introductions. This ensures quality over speed and lets us build the proprietary transaction data that will train our matching engine. As listing volume grows, AI-assisted matching will activate across compatible categories. Every match, regardless of how it is made, requires confirmation from both sides before anything moves.

No. NGOs do not pay to use sur.plus. The platform is funded by Corporate plans; NGOs are partners in the network, not a revenue source. There is no NGO subscription, tier or paywall. We are building an exchange area where verified NGOs pass their own surplus directly to each other - an optional add-on for which a small annual contribution is planned. Matching, logistics and impact documentation stay unaffected and free of charge. Details will follow once they are settled.

Every match generates a structured impact report: item description, quantity, receiving organisation, verified delivery confirmation, calculated avoided CO₂ emissions (GHG Protocol), social resource value (GIIN IRIS+) and ESRS-aligned summary metrics. Exportable as PDF and structured data (JSON/CSV).

sur.plus does not run its own logistics. In most cases, the donating company arranges and covers transport - this is typically still cheaper than commercial disposal, and the cost is offset by savings on storage and disposal fees. We provide guidance on suitable carriers and, for Tree plan customers, can facilitate introductions to partners offering preferential rates for surplus transport. For very local matches (same city or region), NGOs often arrange collection themselves. Where logistics would otherwise prevent a match, we look for a solution together - talk to us before you decline.

What data we collect and why

This website collects no personal data for analytics purposes. No cookies are set and no tracking pixels are used. The following may occur through your use of the site:

  • Live chat - if you write to us in the chat, your messages (and the email address you may optionally add for replies) are stored in our EU-hosted database (processor: Supabase, EU region) so we can answer you. A random conversation ID stays in your browser's local storage so you can see replies on this device - not a tracking cookie, no personal identification. Legal basis: to respond to your request (Art. 6(1)(b)/(f) GDPR). Retention: deleted on request; otherwise after 12 months of inactivity.
  • Impact Calculator & launch list - if you enter your email address (in the Impact Calculator, or in the “notify me at launch” popup), it is used solely to send you your impact report or a one-time launch notification. To deliver these emails we use the processor Resend (Resend, Inc.), configured to send from its EU region (Ireland) on our behalf. Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time. Retention: the Impact Calculator address is not stored after the report is sent; the launch list is deleted once the one-time notification has gone out or you withdraw consent.
